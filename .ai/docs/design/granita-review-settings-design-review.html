Granita 0.14.2  ·  issues #64, #70  ·  the review's two settings, and where a review lives

One rule answers all three asks:
the phone may always type.

A closed laptop is the normal condition, not the error, so no surface here is allowed to be inert when the Mac is away. What is on the phone's screen is what the next copy will contain — that is the promise, and it is the only one that survives an app the system can end at any moment. The Mac is where a review and its settings outlive the phone, which is a different claim from being what the reader is looking at.

From that rule: the phone's Settings screen never refuses to open and never shows a spinner; the diff gains nothing at all; and the review sheet gains exactly one line of type, above the button that matters, because the copy is the moment where knowing changes what a reader does.

Read this before Ask 3  ·  two of the brief's premises have moved at main
The Settings window already has five tabs, and the fifth was this design's own call.
SettingsTab is general, projects, devices, connections, advanced, and GranitaSettingsScreen's own doc comment opens “Design §2 gives it five tabs rather than the specification's four, with Advanced last.” The connection log was lifted out of Advanced because it is read under pressure and because Reset All Data shares that pane. So item 12 is not “do we break a four-tab spec”. That was broken once, deliberately, with a reason, and recorded. The question left is whether this pair of controls earns the same treatment.
General is not port, launch and server state. There is no port row.
GeneralSettingsView holds two sections and three rows: Address, Status, and Open Granita at login. The port field is gone on purpose — macOS chooses the port when Granita advertises, so the control could not be operated. That makes General emptier than the brief assumes, and it does not change my answer: what is left is one coherent subject, this Mac's own address and whether anything is listening, and the wording of a document is not that subject.
The code size is not a setting, whatever §10 says.
DiffPaneLayout fixes it: 11pt on the phone, 12pt beside the selector, and ClientViewerModel.codePointSize is a private var fed from the layout. So the phone's Settings screen has exactly two rows and no third candidate today — which is worth knowing, because it rules out padding the screen with a phone-local group that has nothing in it yet.
The spine  ·  one rule, and the test every state is judged against

What is on screen is what will be copied

Davide's answer on 17 September — the phone keeps a local copy and reconciles, the Mac is the source of truth — reads as a storage rule, and it settles the storage. It does not settle what a screen may claim. So the rule this whole round is drawn from is the narrower one: a Granita surface never shows a value it will not use. A queued opening line is the line the next review begins with. A comment the Mac refused is a comment the copy still contains. The Mac wins at read time, when a fresh device asks what the settings are; the phone wins at write time, because the alternative is discarding something a reader typed on a screen that accepted it.

That rule removes two states before they are drawn. There is no loading state on the phone's Settings screen: it opens on the last values it read, or on the defaults, and both are values it would honour, so there is nothing for a spinner to stand in front of. And there is no blocked copy: the copy button never waits for the Mac, because a review that was never pushed and gets pasted anyway is a complete review in the reader's hand.

If the app is killed right here
Every state below was checked against that question, and one answer changed a drawing. An opening line the reader typed and did not commit is the one thing in this round that can be lost, because a text field's value is not written anywhere until editing ends.
So the field commits on every pause in typing, locally, and sends on end of editing. Local is a defaults write, the same thing the review already does; it costs nothing and it is what makes 1d survivable. A keystroke is never a request — 29 characters would be 29 round trips over a LAN, and the Mac's store already coalesces at one second, which is its debounce and not the wire's.
Everything else here is already safe: a comment is saved when the composer's Save returns, and a review is only destroyed behind Copy and an alert.
Ask 1  ·  first drawing  ·  no baselines exist

The phone's Settings screen

A sheet of stock grouped rows, reached from the toolbar of the screen that already belongs to one Mac, holding two controls and a three-line receipt of what they do. Nothing on it is a new control and nothing on it is a spinner.

The seven states are one screen with one changing footer. That is the whole shape: the controls never move, never disable except in the one state where there is nowhere for a value to go, and the sentence under them says what this phone and that Mac currently disagree about.

The five calls in this ask
a  The way in is the worktree sidebar's toolbar menu, because settings are that Mac's.
b  It is a sheet, not a pushed screen.
c  The default is told by a sentence and by the absence of Reset, not by a placeholder.
d  Unreachable is editable and queued, not read-only.
e  Never paired is reachable and inert, because queueing needs an addressee.

In the toolbar of the screen titled with the Mac's name
The worktree sidebar is already per-Mac: its title is MacBook Pro, its back button returns to the Mac list, and its toolbar menu already holds the arrangement toggle and Pair again. Settings that live on a Mac belong in the menu of the screen that is about that Mac. One item, third: Settings…
REJECTED  ·  A TAB BAR
Two screens do not make a tab bar
The app is a NavigationSplitView inside a stack, and WorktreeSplitScreen's own history is what a container change costs here: a collapsed split view once drew its chrome and none of its rows. A tab bar also takes 49pt of every diff, permanently, for a screen opened twice a year.
REJECTED  ·  THE DIFF'S TOOLBAR
A gear next to “12 files”
The diff screen's toolbar is for reading code, it is where the review capsule and the file count already compete, and it is per-worktree rather than per-Mac. A settings door there would be the fourth thing on a 44pt bar, and the first one that is not about the file in front of the reader.

What is on it

Two controls, and a three-line receipt of what they do

Opening line. One section, one field, full width, holding real editable text rather than a placeholder. Committed locally on a pause and sent on end of editing. It replaces ReviewFeedback.document's first element outright — the parts array's seed, which is the one call site and the one string.

Comment labels. A two-segment picker whose segments are the thing itself: A. B. C. and 1. 2. 3. A popup menu would hide one option behind a tap and show the chosen one as a word; a segmented control shows both idioms at once, which makes the setting its own preview. ReviewIdentifier is already exactly these two cases.

And the receipt. A third section, monospaced, four lines, showing the first two lines of a review and the first comment's head. It exists because the two controls are about a document the reader cannot see from here, and it is the only place both settings are legible together. It is text, not a control, and it is why no Preview button is needed.

And the room for #70. The highlighting theme lands as a fourth section, CODE, below the receipt, and it is this phone's rather than the Mac's: it renders on this screen at this brightness in this appearance, it cannot fail, and it needs no round trip. That divide — the Mac owns what the review says, the phone owns how it draws — is what keeps the screen's footer sentences true when a second group arrives.

Call c  ·  how the default is told
Two signals, and neither is the field's own appearance. The footer sentence states it — “This is the default line.” — and Reset to the default line is absent. A reader who typed something sees the other footer and a Reset row; a reader who has never touched it sees neither.
Rejected: a placeholder. It is the obvious drawing and it is a lie. A placeholder says nothing is set and typing will set it; here the grey words are the exact string that will be exported. Davide's own sentence is “you will see the default one, and you will be able to edit it” — edit, not replace.
Rejected: greying the text. Same lie, and it fails contrast on the one row the reader is about to type into.
Empty is not default. A field cleared to nothing is a review with no opening line, and that is a legal answer — the document then begins with its first comment. Reset is how the default comes back, not the delete key.

Call d  ·  the state that makes this screen hard

The Mac is away: type anyway, and say where it went

Recommendation: editable, queued, and one footer sentence. The controls behave exactly as in 1b. The footer says “MacBook Pro is not reachable. What you change is kept on this phone and sent when it answers.” No banner, no dimming, no lock glyph, and no new row — a row is a control, and there is no control that helps here.

The queued write carries only the fields the reader actually changed, which is the presence-versus-null idiom §8 already spells out for the worktree PATCH. That is what makes editing safe against a Mac whose values this phone has never read: a queued opening line cannot overwrite a label style it never saw.

Killed here, the reader loses nothing. The local commit happens on a pause in typing, before the send, so the queue is on disk. The next launch shows the same value and the same sentence, and the send is retried when the Mac answers — by the same reconnect the diff already does on scenePhase == .active.

Rejected: read-only, last known values. The honest-looking option, and the one I would have drawn first. It fails on frequency: a closed laptop is the normal condition, so read-only means the two controls are unusable most of the time the app is open — including the whole of a train journey spent reading a diff that was already fetched. It also inverts the product's own posture: the reader's phone is the thing that goes away, and this would make the phone the thing that cannot act.

Rejected: refusing to open. An unreachable Mac is not an error, and the day #70 lands the screen also holds something entirely local. A door that is shut because a laptop is shut is a door the reader will find shut on the first try.

Rejected: a “Sync now” button. There is nothing for it to do that reconnecting does not already do, and it would be the one control on the screen that reports on the network rather than on the review. The Connections tab on the Mac is where the network is looked at.

Ask 2  ·  review  ·  40 baselines exist and 39 of them do not change

The composer and the review sheet gain one line of type

The answer to the hard question is only at the copy. Sync state appears in exactly one place — a caption directly above Copy review, in the slot the footer already reserves for a sentence about that button — and nowhere else. Not in the gutter, not on a rail, not on a row, not in the composer.

Your instinct is right and it is right for a stronger reason than noise. The rail is this app's one overloaded object: it is the same 3pt indigo in the gutter, the instruction bar, the composer's anchor and the review row, and its whole job is to say a row here and a mark there are the same thing. A second meaning on it — and this one has not been uploaded — spends the one piece of vocabulary the review feature has on a fact that changes nothing the reader can do while reading code.

And the copy is where it does change something. A reader about to paste into an agent session is deciding whether this document is everything they wrote; a reader about to press Clear is deciding whether it is safe to destroy. Those are the two moments, they are three taps apart, and both are in the sheet.

The composer does not change at all
Its 16 baselines stand. CommentComposerView is 300pt with the keyboard up, holding an anchor, three rows of quotation and a field; its job is one comment, and Save already means written down — the local write is what makes that true and it has not moved. A sync state in the composer would be a report about the previous comment on the screen for writing the next one.
One string in it does change, and only in one state. Nothing — even the refusal in 2e leaves Save alone, because the comment is saved. The Mac is where it survives the phone, and the composer never promised that.

Ask 3  ·  the Mac's settings window  ·  items 12 and 13

A sixth tab, called Review, fourth in the row

Order: General, Projects, Devices, Review, Connections, Advanced. The first four are the pipeline outward — this Mac, what it serves, who may read it, what comes back — then live diagnostics, then the drawer with Reset All Data in it.

And the spec argument is already spent. §9 says four tabs; main ships five, because the connection log was lifted out of Advanced on the grounds that it is read under pressure and must not sit one mis-click from the button that unpairs every device. That departure is in .ai/docs/decisions.md and in SettingsTab's own doc comment. So the question is not whether the four-tab row may be broken. It is whether these two controls earn it on the same terms, and they do: what the review says is its own subject, it is the only pane whose values a phone reads and writes, and it is the first thing in this window that is not about serving.

If the built tab bar overflows at 620pt — six labels, the longest being Connections — widen the window to 680 rather than rename a tab or accept the overflow chevron. The window is a fixed size already, set by the QR in Devices, so widening it is a constant, not a layout change.

What I rejected, and why each is worse
General, as a third section. The tab's own doc comment states its subject: this Mac's address, whether anything is listening, whether Granita comes back after a restart. It is also the pane a reader opens when the phone cannot connect — the worst place to put a text field about the wording of a document. The brief's instinct that it would make the tab about two unrelated things is right, and the code makes it sharper: there is no port row to hide behind, so a review section would be a third of the tab's content.
Advanced. Neither control is advanced — one is a sentence and one is a choice of two — and they would share the pane with Reset All Data. That is the exact objection that moved the connection log out.
Devices. Plausible for ten seconds, since these are the settings phones read. But that pane is who may connect, it is 560pt of QR and countdown, and a text field under a live pairing code is a field nobody will find.
Nowhere: phone only. Rejected because Davide asked for both, and because the Mac is where the value lives — a store that holds a setting no local surface can show is a store you debug with a text editor.

Where a frame and SPEC.md disagree  ·  the spec wins until Davide says otherwise
§9  ·  THE STORE DOCUMENT
Two new fields, and a schema bump
StoreDocument gains reviewSettings (an opening line and a label style) and reviews (per worktree). schemaVersion bumps and the old decoder stays, exactly as §9 instructs. Nothing here needs queries: the phone reads one settings object and one review per worktree, both by key. No disagreement with the LOCKED row — JSON, one actor, atomic replace, all intact.
§9  ·  FALSE AS OF THIS SLICE
“viewedFiles is the only unbounded collection”
Reviews are unbounded in the same way and for a worse reason: a comment carries an excerpt, so a review is kilobytes rather than bytes, and the worktrees they belong to are created and destroyed by an agent. The prune rule and the cap need a sentence of their own in §9, and the Stored reviews row is what makes it observable.
§8  ·  THE WIRE
Four routes, and apiVersion 2
GET/PATCH /v1/settings and GET/PUT /v1/worktrees/{id}/review. PATCH uses §8's own presence-versus-null idiom, which is what makes a queued edit safe. §8 says the client refuses to pair on an apiVersion mismatch and any route 426s on a newer client — so a paired older Mac needs a third answer: 404 on these routes means keep it local, not fail. That is a change to §8's compatibility rule and it is the one I would want written down.
§9  ·  ALREADY DIVERGED
“Settings window, four tabs”
Five ship. A sixth is the same departure again and should be recorded the same way, in .ai/docs/decisions.md, with the window's fixed width noted as the constraint that could overturn it.
§10  ·  PROMISED, NOT BUILT
“the code size is its own setting”
It is a constant in DiffPaneLayout. Two earlier rounds have leaned on that sentence to argue what Dynamic Type may and may not scale. Either §10 loses the claim, or the phone's Settings screen is where the setting lands — in the CODE group, beside #70's theme, as this ask's shape already allows.
§11  ·  STAYS CHEAP
Nothing drawn here builds toward v2
No thread, no reply, no resolve, no history, no second reader, no screen listing past reviews. The union rule in state 10 is the one thing that could be mistaken for multi-reader review, and it is deliberately anonymous: the reader sees comments, not authors, and can delete any of them. REVIEW.md in the worktree, when it comes, reads the same stored review this slice puts on the Mac.
§6's open calls, answered

Placeholders in the opening line: no, and it costs nothing to add later. The stored value is a plain string, so {worktree} can be interpolated at export whenever you want it, with no change to the store or the wire. What v1 would be buying is a template language: escaping for a literal brace, a rule for an unknown token, a rule for an empty value, and a second thing for the field's footer to explain. 0.14.2 dropped the three facts because the session already has them; a reader pasting elsewhere can type the project's name into their own sentence, which is now their sentence.

A sixth tab on the Mac: yes. Fourth position, named Review. The argument is above, and the precedent is the fifth.

A not-yet-synced comment visible at all: only at the copy. One caption above Copy review, per review, with a count. Nothing in the diff, nothing on a rail, nothing in the composer.

Settings reachable with no Mac paired: yes, and inert. Discoverability is the real half of that question; operability is not, because a queued value needs somewhere to go.

What to photograph, and what is a swap of something drawn
Ask 1 asks for four layouts per state, which is 28 frames for a screen whose iPad answer is the same view in a 540 × 620 sheet and whose dark answer is #1C1C1E on #000. Both are stated once and drawn twice rather than seven times, which is how the existing suites are built — ReviewSheetView has 24 baselines for six states, not 96.
New suite, ReviewSettingsViewSnapshotTests: 7 states × iPhone and iPad × light and dark = 28, plus one iPhone frame at xxLarge and one with the keyboard raised. The seven subjects are the states above: loaded, the default, editing, saving, unreachable, refused, never paired.
Existing suites: the composer's 16 do not change. The review sheet's 24 gain four subjects — not yet on the Mac, reconciling, from another device, refused — at 16 new images, and one for each clear-alert wording. The Mac's 30 gain serving-review-light and -dark in GranitaSettingsScreenSnapshotTests plus a ReviewSettingsViewSnapshotTests of four: default, edited, figures, and a store that refused.
The one baseline that will catch a real bug: editing-iPhone. The review sheet's own column baseline was lost four CI runs to a keyboard inset arriving mid-layout, and this screen raises a keyboard on purpose.
